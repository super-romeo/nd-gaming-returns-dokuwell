How it works

Around 350 organizations in North Dakota hold a license for charitable gaming: veterans' posts, fire departments, youth sports associations, service clubs. Most of their gaming revenue comes from pull-tabs sold at bars and other sites.

Every quarter each licensee files a gaming return with the Attorney General. It includes Schedule B1 for every pull-tab game at every site, Schedule B3 for electronic pull-tabs, and figures that must match inventory records. Since July 2026 returns are filed online through the Charitable Gaming Organization Portal.

The portal accepts the return, but the work before it — collecting the numbers from each site and making them agree — still falls on a gaming manager or a volunteer treasurer, often with spreadsheets kept from quarter to quarter.

The service keeps that record as the quarter goes on. Each pull-tab game is entered when it is purchased, when it is put into play at a site and when it is closed, with its serial number, form and results.

Inventory logs follow from these entries: games purchased, games in stock, games in play at each site and games closed. At year end the annual reconciliation is prepared from the same records, so the inventory and the returns tell one story.

Electronic pull-tab results are entered by site and period and summarized for Schedule B3.

Expenses are recorded as they are paid and compared with the 60 percent expense limit throughout the quarter, not only at the end. If spending approaches the limit, you see it while there is still time to act.

At quarter end the service shows the figures for Schedule B1 and Schedule B3 in the order the return asks for them, together with a list of anything missing: a game without a closing entry, a site without results, a number that does not match inventory.

Several people can work in the same organization: a gaming manager, site staff entering results and a treasurer reviewing the totals. Each change is recorded with the person and the date.

Past quarters stay available, so an audit question about an old game or site can be answered from the same place.

Questions

Which schedules does it cover?

Schedule B1 for every pull-tab game at every site and Schedule B3 for electronic pull-tabs, with inventory logs and annual reconciliation.

Does it file the return for us?

No. It prepares the figures; your organization files the return on the Attorney General's online portal.

Can several people use it?

Yes, a gaming manager, site staff and a treasurer, with every change recorded.

Is it connected to the Attorney General?

No. It is an independent service for licensed organizations.

Can we use it already?

Not yet. The service is in preparation, and we are checking whether organizations need it.
